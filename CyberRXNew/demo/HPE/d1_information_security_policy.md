# Hewlett Packard Enterprise — Information Security Policy

**Document ID:** HPE-ISP-001 | **Version:** 4.2 | **Classification:** Internal
**Owner:** Office of the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)
**Effective Date:** 15 January 2026 | **Next Review:** 15 January 2027

---

## 1. Purpose and Objective

The purpose of this Information Security Policy is to establish Hewlett Packard Enterprise's (HPE) overarching commitment to protecting the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information assets that support our global operations and our customers. Our mission is to safeguard the data, systems, and intellectual property underpinning our GreenLake hybrid cloud platform, Aruba networking, ProLiant/Compute portfolio, HPC/Cray systems, and our SAP S/4HANA ERP environment. This objective aligns security outcomes with HPE's business strategy, regulatory obligations, and obligations to shareholders of HPE (NYSE: HPE).

## 2. Scope and Applicability

This policy is organization-wide and applies to all employees, contractors, consultants, interns, and third parties who access HPE information, systems, or facilities. It covers all information assets—whether stored, processed, or transmitted—across every business unit, geography, and technology environment, including on-premises data centers, customer-hosted GreenLake instances, and cloud services. No individual or organizational unit is exempt from its applicability.

## 3. Governance and Program Framework

HPE maintains a formal information security program aligned to the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) and certified against ISO/IEC 27001. Security governance is exercised through a tiered oversight structure. The **Board of Directors**, through its Audit Committee, provides ultimate oversight of cyber risk. An executive **Security Steering Committee**, chaired by the CISO and including representatives from Legal, HR, Engineering, and Finance, sets strategic direction, reviews risk posture, and approves major initiatives. This governance model ensures accountability flows from the board through executive management to operational teams.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities

- **Chief Information Security Officer (CISO):** Owns this policy, leads the security program, and reports security posture to executive management and the Board.
- **Executive Management:** Champions security culture, endorses funding, and integrates risk decisions into business planning.
- **Business and Functional Leaders:** Implement controls within their domains and manage remediation of identified gaps.
- **All Employees:** Comply with this policy, complete required training, and report suspected incidents promptly.

Accountability for information security is shared across all stakeholders, with clearly defined ownership for each control domain.

## 5. Policy Hierarchy and Document Control

This document sits at the top of HPE's security documentation hierarchy. It is supported by subordinate **standards** (mandatory technical requirements), **procedures** (step-by-step operational instructions), and **guidelines** (recommended practices). Version control, revision history, and change logs are maintained for all documents under a formal document control process; each revision is tracked, dated, and attributed within the change log below.

## 6. Awareness, Communication, and Training

This policy is disseminated and published to the workforce via the HPE corporate intranet and remains accessible and available to all personnel at all times. Security awareness and education are mandatory: all new hires complete security training during onboarding, and all employees complete annual refresher training. Ongoing communication campaigns, phishing simulations, and role-based education reinforce a strong security culture and ensure consistent distribution of policy updates.

## 7. Resources and Funding

Executive management commits appropriate resources, budget, and staffing to sustain the security program. The CISO organization is funded with dedicated FTEs across security engineering, governance, risk, threat intelligence, and incident response functions, with annual budget planning aligned to evolving threats and business growth.

## 8. Metrics and Reporting

The effectiveness of the security program is measured through defined key performance indicators (KPIs) and key risk indicators. Metrics—including control coverage, patch compliance, training completion rates, and incident response times—are reported periodically to the Security Steering Committee, executive management, and the Board to inform decision-making.

## 9. Continuous Improvement

HPE is committed to continuous improvement. Findings from audits, incidents, assessments, and lessons learned are analyzed to identify gaps and drive remediation. The program is periodically benchmarked against NIST CSF and ISO 27001 to mature capabilities over time.

## 10. Exceptions and Risk Acceptance

Any deviation from this policy requires a formal exception (waiver) request submitted to the CISO organization. Each request is evaluated for risk, and any residual risk must be documented and formally accepted through risk acceptance by an authorized executive risk owner. Exceptions are time-bound and subject to periodic re-review.

## 11. Enforcement

Compliance with this policy is mandatory. Violations may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment or contract, and may carry additional legal or financial sanctions. Consequences are administered consistently in coordination with Human Resources and Legal, commensurate with the severity of the violation.

## 12. Review and Approval

This policy undergoes annual review and is updated as needed to reflect changes in the threat landscape, regulations, and business operations, following a defined review and update cycle. This document has been reviewed, approved, and authorized by the CISO with endorsement from executive management and the Board Audit Committee.
